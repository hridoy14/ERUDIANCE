## 2. Editorial principles and pillars

2.1. Education first, problem before product, actionable help before CTA, evidence before statistics, human oversight before autonomy. Never do daily direct service promotion. Avoid seven consecutive sales pitches, fabricated testimonials, fake case studies, repetitive generic AI news, fearmongering and unverified savings. A useful post should stand alone even if the reader never buys. Prefer low-cost/manual solutions when appropriate; disclose limitations and when AI is unnecessary. [source: smb-content-strategy-v32.md]
2.2. Content pillars: (1) small-business pains and jobs-to-be-done, (2) practical how-to education and frameworks, (3) buyer questions and objections, (4) problem-solving ideas and process audits, (5) AI literacy, tools and skills, (6) evidence-based research and trends, (7) occasional relevant service/solution awareness. [source: smb-content-strategy-v32.md]
2.3. Every item needs pillar, buyer persona, actual question/pain, cause hypothesis, specific takeaway, funnel stage, evidence status, distinct angle, format and one contextual CTA. Map each item to a documented service category internally, but do not force a service mention in public copy. [source: smb-content-strategy-v32.md]
2.4. Caption honesty rule (never invent customer outcomes, ROI or statistics; do not force a sales pitch on educational posts): single home is `image-rules.md` section 6 rule 6.1. [source: brand-source-of-truth-v36.md]
2.5. No invented ROI, client outcomes, benchmarks, or customer quotes. [source: master-command-v6.md]
2.6. No fabricated clients, case studies, pricing, guarantees or competitors. Block unsupported claims and unverified service offers; report precise gaps and manual work needed. [v33 — source: production-contract-v33.md]
2.7. Do not imply guaranteed outcomes or fabricate customer results. [source: brand-template-contract-v47.md]

## 3. Funnel, cadence and the promotion rule

3.1. Funnel: `Impression → useful save/share → profile or landing page visit → diagnostic/consultation interest → qualified enquiry → meeting → opportunity → customer`. Each transition needs an instrumented event or explicitly marked unknown. CTA is contextual: awareness=save/checklist, consideration=diagnostic audit, decision=consultation. [source: strategy-playbook.md]
3.2. Funnel stages: Awareness: diagnose pains, explain mistakes, questions, concepts, save/share/checklist CTA. Consideration: decision trees, workflow diagrams, checklists, implementation tradeoffs, tool comparisons, audit CTA. Decision: scoped examples, service fit, implementation process, consultation CTA; proof requires verified permission. Retention: customer education only with appropriate audience and evidence. [source: smb-content-strategy-v32.md]
3.3. UTM campaign/source/medium per post if link use authorised; do not fabricate conversion rates. [source: strategy-playbook.md]
3.4. Default 7 content planning slots, not a promise to publish daily; actual publishing cadence is owner-selected and capacity-dependent. As an initial TEST, aim for roughly 4–5 educational/problem-solving slots, 1–2 practical consideration slots, and at most 1 explicit promotional slot in any 7-slot batch; never treat these as universal performance-optimal ratios. No direct-promotion slots when readiness/proof is insufficient. Evaluate actual feedback before adjusting ratios. [source: smb-content-strategy-v32.md]
3.5. Weekly editorial hard rule [v33 — source: production-contract-v33.md]: seven planning slots are not seven mandated publications. Reserve EXACTLY ONE promotional slot per complete editorial week, tied to ONE service category, ONE niche and ONE narrow problem. Other six slots must be educational, Q&A, buyer pain, practical problem solving, AI skills, evidence-based insights, decision education or workflow ideas, with no direct service pitch. Existing Social OS default is four actual posts/week unless owner changes it; the promotional slot is part of those four when approved, never an additional fifth post. If the selected promotion lacks readiness or evidence, status HOLD (do not quietly create a second promotional post). Partial weeks preserve the calendar's single-promotion rule without inventing missing posts.
3.6. CONFLICT — promotion count: rule 3.4 (at most 1 promotional slot as an initial TEST ratio) vs rule 3.5 (EXACTLY ONE promotional slot per complete week, hard rule). [v33 — source: production-contract-v33.md] states v3.3 wins for promotion frequency where older rules conflict, so the hard rule governs; the TEST ratio is preserved here as written. [source: smb-content-strategy-v32.md]
3.7. Acceptance check: fail if promotional count != 1 for a complete seven-slot plan, if any of six educational slots directly sells, if a topic is deleted, if 50 competitors are claimed without 50 grounded records, if employee requirement missing from promotion, if an image is claimed without render proof, if missing evidence passes QA, or if external writes occur without permission. [v33 — source: production-contract-v33.md]
3.8. Day-by-day 30-day plan: Week 1 buyer pain discovery (lead capture, slow ownership, follow-up audit, CRM hygiene, exception routing, weekly recap, audience comments review); Week 2 education (six-stage flow, diagnostic checklist, response SLA, escalation, handoff, measurement, recap); Week 3 solutions (implementation roadmap, automation boundaries, exception handling, human accountability, buyer objection, audit worksheet, recap); Week 4 conversion and proof (approved demonstration, process comparison, qualified CTA, FAQ, decision checklist, buyer case only with verifiable approval, recap and measurement). These are topic prompts, not mandatory daily posting; recommend cadence only after available team capacity and real analytics. Each day has its own content brief and gate chain. [source: strategy-playbook.md]
3.9. Generate a 30-calendar-day plan, with 30 daily slots only when the owner chooses daily posting. Otherwise plan a sustainable cadence and mark non-posting dates. Do not assume that daily posting guarantees growth. Separate calendar dates from publication timestamps; verify account timezone and scheduling constraints. [source: monthly-weekly-social-os.md]
3.10. Balance awareness/consideration/conversion based on business needs, not arbitrary universal percentages. [source: monthly-weekly-social-os.md]

## 4. Buyer research, market research and competitor monitoring

4.1. At the start of a monthly cycle, collect: target sector and role, recurring buyer questions from permitted public sources, customer reviews/forums, search queries, first-party FAQs, customer support signals if authorized, competing offer/positioning examples, buying objections, market maturity, available tools and budget constraints, local market context, and current verified platform guidance. Separate observed evidence from hypothesis. Do not scrape private data, fabricate buyer quotes or claim competitor engagement/conversion without actual measurement. Research sources include original URL, publisher, publication/retrieval date, geography, sample, applicability, reliability and limitations. A vendor study is labeled as such. Research coverage and credibility gates block unsupported statistics and trend claims. [source: smb-content-strategy-v32.md]
4.2. For each candidate idea score on a documented 1–5 rubric: buyer-pain intensity, ICP fit, evidence quality, practical actionability, novelty, service relevance (not promotion), and funnel coverage. These are editorial prioritization scores, not proven sales prediction. Flag duplicate angle and content fatigue. [source: smb-content-strategy-v32.md]
4.3. Buyer-expectation gate: does the content answer the question promised by its hook? Can a small-business owner act without a consultant? Does it avoid overpromising? Does it address objections and limitations? If no, revise. [source: smb-content-strategy-v32.md]
4.4. Each research batch: at least 3 relevant current public examples if available, with publisher, permalink, date, content type, hook, format, CTA, public likes/comments only when observed, and relevance. Add buyer job-to-be-done, counterexample, source credibility, freshness and uncertainty. Trend styles are hypotheses; never copy a competitor's artwork or infer highest conversion from views. Prefer official platform guidance, credible primary studies and direct product/service evidence. [source: strategy-playbook.md]
4.5. Research current public LinkedIn B2B examples from automation consultancies, sales-ops educators, RevOps teams, and service-business operations creators. Examine single-image workflow explainers, diagnostic checklists, process maps, carousels, and product-led visuals. For each useful example record publisher, URL, date, audience, content hook, design format, CTA, and visible metrics if any. Differentiate observation, hypothesis, and measured result. Do not infer lead conversion from likes or claim algorithm preference without credible evidence. If access is unavailable, label MARKET DEMAND UNVERIFIED and proceed with a clearly identified hypothesis. [source: master-command-v6.md]
4.6. Competitor monitoring [v33 — source: production-contract-v33.md]: target 50 unique relevant entities (15 direct, 10 niche, 10 platforms, 10 content benchmarks, 5 emerging); verify names and source links, audit positioning, target buyer, service, actual public LinkedIn examples, creative patterns, objections and content gaps. If fewer than 50 verified, report exact count and missing coverage; never fill with invented competitors. No copying competitor posts or images.
4.7. Topic preservation [v33 — source: production-contract-v33.md]: no destructive deletes; preserve ID, history and provenance. READY, ENRICH, RESEARCH_NEEDED or HOLD; duplicate suggestions are non-destructive links, not deletion.
4.8. Buyer intelligence mapping: map verbatim observations to buyer roles, pains, job-to-be-done, evidence strength (OBSERVED, CORROBORATED, VALIDATED_FOR_ICP). Do not infer market prevalence from anecdotes. [source: workflow-v3.md]
4.9. Research controller: reject fabricated/unlinked sources and unsupported statistics; NOT_VERIFIED blocks factual claims. [source: workflow-v3.md]
4.10. Source discovery: search only authorized public pages and connected first-party sources; store source_id, URL, publisher, published_at, retrieved_at, exact supported claim, credibility, limitations. No private scraping or platform bypass. [source: workflow-v3.md]

## 6. Monthly strategy cycle (M0)

6.1. Monthly strategy cycle starts on each calendar month's 21st and ends the following month's 20th, inclusive. Plan month-specific buyer themes and content opportunities once per cycle; refresh evidence, questions and performance at each weekly production session without recreating the monthly strategy unnecessarily. On a partial cycle, plan remaining dates only. Month length varies: do not force every cycle to exactly 30 days. The 21st is a planning boundary, NOT proof of an active scheduler; automation requires an actual connected scheduled task. [source: smb-content-strategy-v32.md]
6.2. Monthly strategy gate M0: verify current business objectives, ICP, offers, landing page, available proof, audience geography, LinkedIn policies, brand assets, Social OS capabilities and prior performance. Do not invent analytics. Define measurable hypotheses: qualified leads, website clicks, consultation requests, saves, relevant comments, and impressions as diagnostic only. Document evidence URLs, dates and confidence. Produce 30-day calendar: content_id, local_date, week, pillar, buyer stage, buyer pain, claim/evidence, hook, post copy, visual thesis, format, CTA, landing page UTM, metric target as hypothesis, status, dependencies. Controller must approve monthly plan before batch production. [source: monthly-weekly-social-os.md]
6.3. Required outputs — monthly strategy with source registry, ICP/persona hypotheses, pain and question clusters, opportunity scores, funnel assumptions, content calendar and experiment plan. Weekly 7-slot review batch with content ID/date/pillar/persona/question/pain/hook/answer/format/funnel/CTA/evidence/service map/status. Publish-ready claims only after independent evidence, brand and asset QA. Report PASS/BLOCKED/NOT_VERIFIED per gate. All drafts are read-only by default. [source: smb-content-strategy-v32.md]
6.4. Monthly calendar record fields: dates, platform(s), topic/title, pillar/funnel, primary format, derivatives, SEO and LinkedIn keyword targets, source references, CTA, status, campaign, UTM. [source: workflow-v3.md]
6.5. Track useful saves, meaningful comments, qualified profile/site clicks, enquiries, calls booked and sales when instrumentation exists; do not infer leads from impressions or invent analytics. Compare hypotheses with actual observed data and revise the next weekly batch. [source: smb-content-strategy-v32.md]

## 10. Workflow v3 pipeline, state machine and failure logic

10.1. Scope and execution policy [source: workflow-v3.md]: master action `@Erudience linkedin image` accepts optional `topic`, `category`, `month`, `platforms`, `formats`, `cadence`, `brand`, `model_preference`, `batch_size`, `approval_scope`, `output_mode`. Defaults are proposals, not evidence. No remote write without explicit authorization. For each input, validate schemas, source provenance, claims, account access and brand assets.
10.2. First-to-last pipeline [source: workflow-v3.md]: 1 INPUT_SCHEMA: reject unknown categories/unsupported formats and malformed dates. 2 SOURCE_DISCOVERY (see section 4 rule 4.10). 3 BUYER_INTELLIGENCE (see section 4 rule 4.8). 4 RESEARCH_CONTROLLER (see section 4 rule 4.9). 5 MONTHLY_CALENDAR (see section 6 rule 6.4). 6 WEEKLY_REFRESH (see section 7 rule 7.3). 7 CONTENT_WRITER (see section 9 rule 9.1). 8 CONTENT_CONTROLLER (see section 9 rule 9.2). 9 CREATIVE_BRIEF: for each approved content item, infer headline, visual thesis, hierarchy, composition, required brand assets, copy locks, accessible contrast and dimensions. 10 PROMPT_ENGINEER: build structured positive and negative instructions with source references and immutable copy; compare prompt hash to brief hash. 11 FORMAT_ROUTER: text-only -> no image; image/flowchart/poster -> renderer; carousel/document -> multi-page document pipeline, never claim a single PNG equals a carousel. 12 IMAGE_QA: mechanical checks, human/vision visual review, text accuracy, brand provenance, image-copy alignment; max 3 bounded repairs per stage then ESCALATE. 13 BATCH_CONTROLLER: all requested items must pass individually; batch failures cannot be hidden by average score. 14 OWNER_REVIEW: explicit per-item or enumerated batch approval; record exact hashes. 15 SOCIAL_OS_HANDOFF: require separate write permission and verified endpoint/connector. Create draft and media, then read back exact record. Scheduling requires separate explicit authorization. Do not guess URLs or credentials. 16 ANALYTICS_LOOP: only actual published metrics; measure qualified engagements, UTM clicks, enquiries and meetings; update next weekly hypotheses without fabricated outcomes.
10.3. State machine [source: workflow-v3.md]: PLANNED -> RESEARCHED -> EVIDENCE_VERIFIED -> WRITTEN -> CONTENT_APPROVED -> BRIEF_APPROVED -> PROMPT_APPROVED -> RENDERED -> VISUAL_VERIFIED -> OWNER_APPROVED -> UPLOAD_AUTHORIZED -> REMOTE_VERIFIED -> SCHEDULED_VERIFIED (if separately authorized).
